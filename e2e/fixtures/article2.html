Why offline reading matters

By Sam Lee

Offline reading changes how you treat saved articles. Instead of a pile of links that need a network, you get a quiet library that is always there, on the train or on a plane.

Highlights are the second half of the habit. A sentence you marked is far easier to find again than a vague memory of an article you once liked.

Exports keep you in control. Markdown files and a plain JSON backup mean that no single service can take your reading history away.

Finally, keep the queue short. Archive what you have read, delete what you never will, and the list stops feeling like homework.

A short checklist

  1. Save the article.
  2. Read it offline.
  3. Highlight one idea.
